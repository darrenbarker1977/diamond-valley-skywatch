Upcoming stops
    How this mission adapts

    Sky checks run every minute while the app is visible. Fresh weather, a new observing location, a different planning time or changes to your eyepieces rebuild the upcoming stops. Your current target stays selected when recommendations change.

    Targets need darkness, a useful altitude and safe separation from the Sun. The Moon can rule out faint targets. Poor or missing weather never removes the tour: conditions are shown as advice, and clearer stop times are preferred when possible. Confirm actual conditions outdoors.

    Offline tours use the last downloaded forecast and show its age. Without weather for your location or date, the route still uses calculated sky positions and clearly marks weather unavailable. Before dusk, the live tour shows an evening start; Find now stays unavailable until a target is suitable in the actual sky.

    Why doesn’t the map move with my phone?

    Live time and phone movement are different. The map updates star positions for the selected time and location. By default, you move the view by dragging it. The small compass reads your phone’s heading separately; starting it does not turn on map-follow.

    1. After dusk, open Pointing, confirm your actual location and enable sensors.
    2. Choose handheld or telescope-mounted mode, then identify and record one suitable object. A second object improves the alignment.
    3. Return to this map and tap Follow aligned phone. Handheld mode follows the rear-camera direction; mounted mode follows the phone’s top edge along the telescope tube.

    Dragging the map, centring a target or choosing a compass direction returns to manual view. Brief gaps pause the map and it resumes automatically when fresh aligned readings return. Follow remains on while you move; the telescope arrows still wait for steadier readings. Losing alignment, changing phone placement or leaving live mode stops Follow and requires setup again. Planned skies do not use phone-follow. An indoor compass test alone cannot align this map to the sky.

    Map controls & observing location

    Drag to explore. Pinch or use + / − to zoom. Phone follow becomes available after aligning on one identified object; a manually explored map is not a pointing measurement.

    How the pulses guide you

    Pulses get faster as the aligned phone estimate approaches the selected target. A gentle double pulse means within the coarse 3° estimate; it does not confirm centring. Check the finder, then the eyepiece. Smooth movement can keep the pulses going while arrows wait for steadier readings. Vibration pauses when readings are stale, noisy or too fast, alignment is lost, you leave Find it, or the app is hidden. It works in handheld and mounted modes when supported by the phone.
