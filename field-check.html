Your first night outside

Use Barker After Dark anywhere you choose. Browsing and planning need no alignment, camera or pointing sensors. Align only after dusk, while physically at the observing site shown in the app.

Start with the phone in your hand
  1. Choose your observing location on Home. Use precise location, search a place, or enter coordinates. In Pointing, confirm that you are physically there. Wait until after dusk; daytime alignment is deliberately unavailable.
  2. Open Navigator → Pointing, choose In my hand · point the rear camera, and tap Enable sensors. Use the sensor help if permission or readings are unavailable.
  3. Choose one easy object you can positively identify. The app suggests suitable bright objects for your location and time, including the Moon, Venus or Polaris when eligible. Clouds and obstacles can still hide them; never guess the identity.
  4. Point the rear camera toward that object. The phone's top edge is not the handheld aiming direction. The optional rear-camera view keeps its crosshair, confirmation and Record controls together. If video cannot show the object clearly, close it and choose something you can identify confidently.
  5. Centre the object, hold still for about one second, tick the confirmation and record it. One target starts basic guidance. Accuracy is unverified. A second target refines the fit; a third independent check is optional.
  6. Find it opens automatically after recording. The live map follows your aligned phone. Choose another visible target and follow the arrows; vibration starts too when supported, unless you have switched it off. Your off choice is remembered. Faster pulses mean closer to the aligned phone estimate; a gentle double pulse means inside the coarse 3° estimate. Confirm the actual object yourself. You can change targets without repeating alignment while the session remains valid.

Use the telescope straps
  1. Choose Strapped to my 250P in Pointing. Changing between handheld and mounted modes clears the previous alignment because they use different aiming directions.
  2. Strap the phone securely outside the tube, screen facing outward. Its physical top edge, opposite the charging port, must point toward the tube opening, with the phone's long edges parallel to the tube. Confirm it cannot slide or rotate. Keep the telescope base stable and magnetic accessories away.
  3. Enable sensors. Centre one positively identified object in a low-power eyepiece and record it. For the Moon, use the middle of the disk. One target starts basic estimates; a second refines them and a third check is optional.
  4. Use Find it and Scope view for larger controls. Finish through the telescope finder and eyepiece. If the phone slips or directions look wrong, stop and align again; a steel tube can disturb magnetic readings even with tight straps.

Motion, camera & location permission help

Chrome on Android: open the site's controls beside the address, then Permissions / Site settings. Allow Motion sensors for pointing, Camera for optional aiming video, and Location only if you want a device location fix. Menu names vary by Chrome and Android version.

Android Settings → Apps → Chrome → Permissions also controls camera and location. For the installed app, check that app's permissions too. A website cannot grant itself permission or change Android settings for you.

If location is denied or has no signal, keep the last selected site or enter a place/coordinates on Home. If absolute orientation is unsupported, browsing, charts, forecasts and tours still work; measured direction arrows cannot be supplied.

Camera or sensor readings are missing

The camera is optional and does not recognise objects. Nothing is recorded or uploaded. Use Continue without camera if the preview is unavailable. For a strapped phone, centre the object through the telescope's low-power eyepiece instead.

For stale sensor readings, hold still briefly and check the current status. Check permissions, remove magnetic cases and return to the app. Brief gaps keep the alignment and resume guidance when fresh readings return. Use sensor controls to retry if continuity is lost. Home and Forecast inside this app keep a valid foreground session; they do not require starting again. Never substitute the compass alone for alignment. Losing sensor continuity, changing location or phone placement, leaving live mode, or returning after the app was hidden may require a fresh alignment.

Vibration depends on browser and phone support. If unavailable or blocked, use the arrows; vibration does not prove that an object is centred.

Prepare for no cell service

While connected, choose the destination, open Forecast and load the app. The last selected location and downloaded weather stay on this browser where storage is available. Saved weather is labelled with its age and is not a live update. Sky positions calculate locally from the bundled catalogue.

Aurora, atmospheric and ISS feeds need their own downloaded data. Old or unavailable data remains labelled unavailable; the app cannot create a fresh forecast or orbit offline. An ISS prediction is suppressed when its orbit data is too old.

Keep the screen hold on while using the app. If Android refuses it, increase Display → Screen timeout manually. Screen hold does not override the power button or keep pointing valid after the app is hidden.
