# First outdoor check: Pixel 8 and Skywatch Navigator

Open the permanent Diamond Valley Skywatch website directly in Chrome on your
Pixel. From Home, choose Point my phone / telescope. The private preview remains
available separately for staging. Sensors require HTTPS; they stay opt-in.

## Start in your hand

1. Stay at the Diamond Valley observing site shown on the page. Wait until
   after dusk and choose a clear patch of sky. Lower the phone brightness.
2. On Home, choose **Point my phone / telescope** (or open the Navigator’s **Pointing** tab), choose **In my hand**, and tap **Enable sensors**.
   If Chrome asks, allow motion/orientation sensors. If it reports no usable
   readings, stop here and keep the error message for troubleshooting.
3. The guide suggests an easy object for the current sky. Use its directions
   and identification hints. If you cannot confidently identify it, choose
   another option. The Moon is often easiest when it is up and bright enough;
   Venus and Polaris are offered only when suitable. Clouds are not detected.
4. Optionally tap **Show rear-camera aiming view** and allow camera access.
   Centre the identified object on the crosshair. The rear camera points toward
   the object; the top of the phone is not the handheld aiming direction.
   No photo or video is recorded. If the camera cannot show the star clearly,
   choose a brighter object or another night rather than guess.
5. Hold still, tick the confirmation, and record. Follow the guide for a
   second object in another part of the sky, then a third object to check it.
   The guide keeps arrows off until all three steps succeed.
6. Use **Objects** to search for an easy target, then move slowly. A compact guidance line remains visible there while sensors are active. Check that the directions
   agree with the actual sky. “Within 3°” is a rough area, not a promise of
   centering. Try several parts of the sky. Stop if the directions disagree.

## Then try the telescope straps

1. Stop sensors. Choose **Strapped to my 250P**. Use a stable base.
2. Strap the phone along the outside of the tube, screen outward. Its physical
   top edge, opposite the charging port, must point toward the tube opening;
   the phone's long edges must run parallel to the tube. Arbitrary angles are
   not supported. Check it cannot slide or rotate and that the telescope can
   move freely. Keep magnetic cases/accessories away.
3. Confirm placement and enable sensors. All previous alignment is discarded.
   Centre each suggested object in your low-power eyepiece, then record it.
   For the Moon, centre the middle of the disk. Complete two objects and the
   independent third-object check as instructed.
4. Use the finder first, then a low-power eyepiece, when following arrows.
   If the phone slips or a direction looks wrong, stop and align again.
   The steel tube may prevent reliable compass readings even with tight straps.
   A failed check means guidance is unavailable, not that you should force it.

## What to report

Record the Chrome/Android version, handheld or strapped mode, date/time,
objects used, fit residual and third-object check error, and whether directions
agreed with additional objects elsewhere in the sky. Record the exact error
message if a permission, sensor or camera step failed. Do not publish raw
sensor streams or precise personal location information to diagnose this.

A passing three-object check is only one local check. Real Pixel 8 / telescope
performance remains unverified until these outdoor checks succeed. Use your finder and low-power eyepiece to finish locating targets.
